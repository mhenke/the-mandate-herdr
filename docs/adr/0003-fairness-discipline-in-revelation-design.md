# Fairness discipline in revelation design

A political thriller whose twist recontextualizes the player's own actions can cheat in two ways: reveal information the player could never have had, or punish trust the fiction never warned about. We decided the story's central revelation (the player's early verification note becomes the third count that certifies the emergency) must be built entirely from fair-play artifacts — documents the player sees at the moment of action, which later re-read with new meaning. No decisive clue is introduced after the reveal, and no obvious-on-its-face fact (like an unratified watermark) is withheld from competent characters to save it for the player.

## Considered options

- **A. Fair-play recontextualization (chosen).** The submission acknowledgment at filing time shows a blank origin field and a retraction right; the counting line arrives on the next intake receipt; re-reads reinterpret rather than ambush. The trap is procedural and the player had the evidence to suspect it from L4.
- **B. Withheld-document twist (rejected).** An earlier draft hid the decisive fact (a "NOT RATIFIED" watermark) from every competent character until the player requested it. Rejected: implausible withholding, and the reveal punished no player error — it just delayed the plot.
- **C. Gotcha reveal (rejected).** Explaining the stripping mechanism only after the player's forwarding mattered would be a late ambush; the design requires the on-screen artifact visible at the moment of the action.

## Consequences

- Every major clue gets an audit row: first visible date, payoff level, and the alternative reading preserved until a specific observation excludes it.
- The twist stress test is part of story acceptance: the early guesser must be rewarded, the wrong theories must be cleanly disproven by evidence the player holds, and no mastermind appears after the law is read.
- Failures stay procedural and re-triable; the design never converts a narrative surprise into an unwinnable state.
